Major and minor are different maps

Major ii–V–I

In C: Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7. Minor seventh, dominant seventh, tonic major seventh.

Minor iiø–V–i

In C minor: Dm7♭5 → G7 → Cm. Half-diminished seventh, a major-third dominant, and minor tonic.

The ø symbol means half-diminished seventh, not fully diminished seventh. The B natural inside G7 in C minor comes from minor-key harmonic practice and leads powerfully to C.

The musical move

Make the chord change almost invisible

Guide tones in C major

Dm7: F + CG7: B + FCmaj7: E + B
  1. Play Dm7 for one measure, G7 for one measure, Cmaj7 for two measures. Don't rush the third chord.
  2. Try the Auto smooth voicing control, then reproduce each suggested shape on your keyboard.
  3. Sing one long note through the first two chords, then resolve your melody on E over Cmaj7.
  4. Repeat in C minor. Hear how G7's B natural wants to resolve upward to C.

Once the sound is familiar, play only the root, third and seventh where appropriate. These are shells, economical jazz accompaniment textures for your 25-key instrument.
