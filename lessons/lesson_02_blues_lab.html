Play a blues form you can sing over.

Learn the harmonic jobs of I, IV and V, feel the 12-bar cycle, and answer a sung phrase with three or four expressive notes.

20–30 minutes
Five-minute starter

I–IV–V is a musical address

On guitar you already know the blues sound. On keys, the same relationship becomes three chord shapes. For C blues: C7 (I7), F7 (IV7), G7 (V7). For G blues with your C harmonica: G7, C7, D7.

A major-key blues often uses dominant sevenths on I, IV and V. In a minor blues, this course starts with minor sevenths on i, iv and v; a major dominant V7 can be chosen separately as a turnaround color.

Play it, then sing

A twelve-bar campfire exercise

  1. Set the key to G and play twelve slow bars as full chords, one chord per bar. Count aloud: one, two, three, four.
  2. Repeat using short chord stabs on beats 1 and 3. Leave the other beats open for your voice.
  3. Sing a two-bar phrase, then answer it using G–B♭–C–D (or another small subset of G blues). Repeat with variation.
  4. On the final V chord (D7), aim for F♯ or C, then resolve toward a note in G7. Hear the change in tension.
Your C diatonic harmonica: G blues is the classic second-position cross-harp context. A holder is useful if you want simultaneous keys and harmonica; it is also completely musical to alternate them.
