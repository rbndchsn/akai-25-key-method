04 / melodic vocabulary

Fewer notes.
More meaning.

Use scales, chord tones and arpeggios to build short melodic replies to your own singing.

Outcome

Make melodies from the right few notes.

Scales provide a pool of possibilities; arpeggios outline a chord. Use both to make short responses between sung phrases, not to perform nonstop exercises for an imaginary examiner.

15–25 minutes
Five-minute starter

The notes belong to different jobs

C blues scale

C–E♭–F–G♭–G–B♭. Six notes. Let the G♭ blue note color the path to G.

Cmaj7 arpeggio

C–E–G–B. Four chord tones. These strongly imply the harmony even without accompaniment.

Play less, say more

Three musical response drills

  1. Choose G blues. Play G–B♭–C–D, then pause for a full beat. Answer your own phrase by descending D–C–B♭–G.
  2. Switch to the blues V chord arpeggio (D7 in G). Hear how F♯ leads toward G. Play one four-note ascending idea, then stop.
  3. Sing a line first. Answer with only three keyboard notes. Change the rhythm and contour, not the number of notes.
Improvisation is not a race up and down the scale. The impact comes from chord-tone targets, phrasing, articulation, rhythmic placement, tension and silence.
