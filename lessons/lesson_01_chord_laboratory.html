Outcome

One hand can carry a whole song.

Know the major/minor difference, find functional seventh chords, and use inversions so each chord change needs less movement. Begin with the C–Am–F–G arrangement and sing over it.

15–25 minutes
Five-minute starter

Only one note changes

C major: C E G

Play C–E–G together. Listen to the brighter major third, E.

C minor: C E♭ G

Lower only E to E♭. Root and fifth stay. That's the minor color.

Guitar translation: the major/minor distinction you know from fretboard shapes is the same third interval on keys. A major third spans four semitones; a minor third spans three.

Practice in one hand

Four chords with minimal travel

Instead of changing from root-position shape to root-position shape, use these compact voicings. They sit close together and leave the other hand available for your filter knob or Favorites.

ChordNotes, low to highMusical goal
CC–E–GStart stable

FC–F–AKeep C and A, raise E to F

Three ways to accompany your voice

  1. Hold each chord for four beats; sing a simple melody on top.
  2. Strike each chord on beats 1 and 3; leave room between attacks.
  3. Play a chord on beat 1, then a short two- or three-note response after the sung line.
