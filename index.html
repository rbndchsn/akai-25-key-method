Welcome to the workshop

A small keyboard. A complete musical vocabulary.

You're a musician, not a beginner to music. These five lessons transfer what you already know from guitar to a new physical layout: chords, voice-leading, blues, jazz and short melodic responses. The point is to write and perform songs, not to sight-read nursery tunes.

Approach

Learn the *instrument*, not piano conventions

Right hand: harmony

Play three- and four-note shapes, rhythmic pulses, stabs and occasional short lead lines. The voice carries the melody.

Left hand: expression

Use a free hand for changing Favorites, filter sweeps and musical punctuation. Use two hands on keys when the song calls for it.

25 keys is a register

Our diagrams deliberately show two octaves of chromatic notes from C to C. Each diagram has a separate visual octave selector. It does not change the Akai until you press its physical OCTAVE keys.

Keep it honest

The Play mk3 does not create an entire chord from one keypress, nor does it record multi-layer loops on its own. Every exercise is meant to be played by your fingers and heard with your ears.

First 10 minutes

An immediate musical experiment

  1. Choose an electric piano or organ patch on the Akai. Keep the filter open enough for clear sound.
  2. Open Lesson 01 and select C major: C–E–G. Play one chord, then leave a full beat of silence.
  3. Sing one short improvised phrase. Respond with a chord, not with a melody fighting your voice.
  4. Try C → Am → F → G with nearby voicings. This is already the basis of a song.
